# Getting support, and reporting harm

Who to talk to about harassment, discrimination, sexual violence or hazing, and
what each of them has to do with what you tell them.

Health, counselling and everyday wellbeing are on
[health and wellness](wellness.md). This page covers harm.

Three of those lines run 24/7: Tang counselling, the Crisis Text Line and the
Family Violence Law Center. The Tang Center also takes walk-ins for urgent
counselling on weekdays, without an appointment.

"Unstated" means the training does not say either way, so ask them directly
before relying on it.

!!! warning "Most staff have to report a disclosure"
    OASIS advisors and most professional staff are **responsible employees**. If
    you tell them about sexual violence or sexual harassment, they must report it
    to OPHD, who will then contact you about your rights and options. Sometimes
    that is what you want. PATH to Care and the Ombuds Office are confidential, so
    start there if you want to think first.

## What reporting starts

**OPHD** takes anonymous consultations, and it can arrange **supportive
measures** such as academic or housing adjustments without a formal resolution.
The **Center for Student Conduct** handles hazing and other conduct, and the UC
whistleblower hotline is a second route for hazing.

Most channels accept a report without a name. Doing so narrows what the campus can
do in response. Either way, a reporter's identity is kept private as far as the
process allows.

!!! warning "Online reports get no immediate response"
    Nobody monitors the web reporting forms continuously. Call **510-642-3333**
    from campus, or 911, if someone is in danger.

## If someone tells you they've been harmed

You do not need a script, and you are not expected to handle it alone.

- **Listen**, somewhere quiet and private if you can get there.
- **Say you believe them.** "Thank you for telling me." "I'm sorry this happened
  to you." "It's not your fault."
- **Leave the investigation alone.** Establishing what exactly happened is
  someone else's job, and asking why they were there or what they were wearing
  does harm.
- **Let them decide what happens next:** whether to report, tell anyone else, or
  seek counselling.
- **Offer rather than push.** "Would you like to call PATH to Care together?"
  helps more than a list of offices.
- **Get support for yourself**, and ask their consent before discussing it with
  anyone for that purpose. PATH to Care supports the people supporting survivors.

If you say the wrong thing, say so and carry on: "Sorry, that sounded like I don't
believe you. I do, and I'm here."

## Hazing

Hazing does not require anyone to object at the time. Berkeley's Code of Student
Conduct defines it as an act committed in the context of joining or staying in a
student organization, **"regardless of the willingness of such other person or
persons to participate"**, that creates a risk of physical or psychological
injury, including personal degradation or disgrace, beyond the ordinary risks of
taking part.

California's **Matt's Law** allows felony prosecution where serious injury or
death results, and it applies to non-students as well. Federal law makes hazing a
Clery-reportable crime. Policy and reporting routes:
<https://deanofstudents.berkeley.edu/our-resources/support/hazing/>.

Three questions from the training, for an activity you are unsure about. Would we
be in trouble if an administrator walked past? Have we been asked to keep it
secret? Is anyone being hurt or humiliated?

## Discrimination, harassment and speech

Discrimination means adverse or unequal treatment because of a protected identity
that interferes with your ability to take part in University programs. Harassment
means conduct that is unwelcome, based on a protected identity, and **severe,
persistent or pervasive** enough to create an intimidating or offensive
environment. The protected-category list and the standards OPHD applies are at
<https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/1001004/Anti-Discrimination> and
<https://ophd.berkeley.edu/policies-and-procedures/policy-and-procedures-students>.
They are precise and they get amended, so use them in place of a summary.

Berkeley's position on speech is worth quoting:

> The Constitution prohibits UC Berkeley, as a public institution, from banning
> or punishing speech based on its content or viewpoint. […] Protected speech can
> cause a hostile environment. However, if speech is protected, we can't shut it
> down. We have to use other avenues to address the harm caused.

Those avenues include OPHD supporting the person harmed, and the campus or the
org speaking for itself. Hate and bias incidents can be reported at
<https://ophd.berkeley.edu/topics/hatebias-incident>.

## If you're a GSA officer

Two things change when you hold a signatory role.



**You are not a responsible employee.** If a member discloses sexual violence or
harassment to you as a signatory, you have no obligation to report it, and the
decision stays with them.

!!! warning "Check your other roles"
    The training addresses the signatory role alone. The same person as a **GSI,
    GSR or other academic appointee** may hold responsible-employee status in that
    capacity, and holding a GSA office does not change that. Check
    <https://svsh.berkeley.edu/responsible-employee> if you hold an appointment.

**Handle the incident through the campus process rather than inside the
organization.**

- **Leave the question of whether an incident happened to OPHD.**
- **Take no action against a member without guidance from OPHD**, including
  removal or suspension. An org that disciplines someone outside the SVSH process
  creates Title IX and VAWA exposure for itself.
- **Discuss specifics** only with people who need to know, or with the
  survivor's agreement.

What to do instead: connect them to PATH to Care, get support for yourself from
the same place, contact OPHD about supportive measures or options, anonymously if
you prefer, tell the OASIS advisor, and ask the person what they need. In the
following weeks, check in, and make sure everyone honours whatever they asked for.

Build prevention in rather than improvising it. Violence prevention and conduct
expectations can go into the bylaws, and ours are still a
[stub](../about/bylaws.md). Behaviour expectations for social events can go into
the invitation. Campus peer education programs run bystander workshops on
request; ask whether a given workshop is open to graduate students, since most are
written for undergraduates.
