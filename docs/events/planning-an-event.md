# Planning an event

What Berkeley requires for an event, in the order you meet it. Most GSA events are
a room, some food and thirty people, and clear nearly all of this in one step. The
requirements that do apply have lead times measured in weeks, so read the list
before choosing a date.

## The order of operations

1. **Check that the org is current:** active in CalLink, and you an accepted
   signatory. See
   [registration and signatories](../about/registration-and-signatories.md).
   Nothing below works otherwise.
2. **Find the money.**
   [Funding events](../about/funding-events.md) covers CPH's own fund and
   the campus sources.
3. **Book the space.** This has the earliest hard deadline.
4. **Work out whether the event counts as high-risk:** insurance, minors, food,
   waivers.
5. **Publicise it**, with accessibility information on the invitation.

## Where to hold it

| Option | Cost | Lead time and limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Eshleman and bNorth conference rooms** | Free | Same-day booking allowed. Capped per day and per semester per org |
| **Eshleman practice and performance spaces** | Free | Same-day allowed. A few hours a day, capped per semester |
| **General Assignment classrooms**, via 25Live | Usually free | **Single room: at least 7 days ahead. Multiple rooms: at least 6 weeks.** Weekday booking opens **1 July** for Fall and **1 December** for Spring |
| **Event Services venues:** Pauley Ballroom, Anna Head, outdoor plazas | Rental charges | Up to **a year** ahead. A purchase order has to exist on our CalLink page before they confirm |
| **Cub-E tabling**, Upper Sproul | Free, tables and chairs included | First-come, first-served, weekdays 9–5, no reservations. **Lottery in the first two weeks** of each semester |

GA classrooms cost nothing and carry the most restrictions. Event Services
schedules them first-come, first-served by submission time, closes them on
academic holidays, and requires a fresh request after a denial. Offer alternate
dates and rooms in the first request. In weeks 1 to 4 of the semester, bookings
start at 8 PM. Two rooms suit events involving movement or performance. One
person should own classroom bookings for the year.

!!! failure "No food, no amplified sound, no moving the furniture"
    Food and drink, amplified sound and moving the furniture are all prohibited.
    An event that needs any of them belongs in a Student Union space, where
    catering goes through an approved caterer and you specify the setup at
    booking. In either place, a space left damaged or dirty is billed to the org,
    and **the signatories are held responsible**.

Rate sheets and venue pricing change yearly. Get current figures from Berkeley
Event Services (`eventservices@berkeley.edu`, 510-642-1141) or classroom
scheduling (`classroomevents@berkeley.edu`), and see
<https://eventservices.berkeley.edu/>.

!!! danger "Cancel in writing, and early"
    Event Services charges a share of the rental on a sliding scale that reaches
    **100% within two weeks** of the event, plus anything already spent on our
    behalf. GA classrooms require written cancellation at least **3 business
    days** ahead for a weekday booking and **14** for a weekend one. A late
    cancellation costs us the full reservation fee for a room nobody used.

    Misuse and no-shows run on a three-warning system: a written warning, then a
    referral to the Center for Student Conduct, then a second referral and
    possible **loss of booking privileges across every campus venue**. A severe
    incident goes straight to the referral.

## Event liability insurance

This is cover for the event itself, and it is separate from the health insurance
on the [wellness page](../life/wellness.md).

UC's Office of the President insures **most on-campus RSO events**, provided the
org holds current registration and good standing. Low-risk events (meetings,
lectures, receptions, seminars) are **covered automatically** and need no action.

High-risk events need additional cover. The categories are:

- anyone **under 18** attending
- animals
- sports or athletic activity
- **performances of any kind**
- moving vehicles
- tools, weapons or heavy equipment
- **fire, including cooking**
- high attendance

!!! warning "Off-campus events are not covered"
    UCOP insures on-campus events. Off-campus venues often require proof of
    insurance, and that cover comes out of the org's own money. Budget for it
    before booking the restaurant.

Apply for a certificate of insurance through CampusConnexions
(<https://ucberk.campusconnexionsuc.com/student-campus-groups/registered-student-organization/event-liability.html>)
and allow a **minimum of 7–10 business days** per event. Underwriters often email
questions, and slow answers are the usual cause of a late certificate. Risk
Services publishes a risk worksheet at
<https://riskservices.berkeley.edu/plan-event>.

Hazardous materials and equipment, from soldering irons and 3D printers to
chemicals and lasers, require an email to `oasis.center@berkeley.edu` **before**
the session. Permission for an individual student to work in a lab or makerspace
does not extend to org activity, because the requirements for an RSO differ.

## Events that need six weeks' notice

Berkeley's **Major Events Policy** applies to an event that meets any one of
these conditions:

- **300 or more expected attendees**
- a dance, or a concert with amplified sound where most of the audience stands
- **alcohol served**
- **outdoor amplified sound**
- campus officials judge that it could significantly affect campus safety,
  security or services

!!! danger "Six weeks, and no late submissions"
    Deciding whether the event qualifies is **our** responsibility. A qualifying
    event needs three documents **six weeks before** the date: the Major Events
    registration form, the UCPD event notification and security assessment, and
    proof of a venue reservation request. Berkeley accepts no late submissions and
    cancels the reservation for non-compliance. Send questions to
    `majorevents@berkeley.edu` and copy the OASIS advisor.

Undergraduate RSOs may not serve alcohol at all. We are a graduate org, so
alcohol is possible, though it puts the event into the Major Events process.
Campus policy separately prohibits promoting any particular alcohol or tobacco
**brand** in publicity, on campus or off.

## Anyone under 18

An event involving minors who are not Berkeley students requires **a meeting with
the OASIS advisor**, covering mandatory reporting, background checks, guardian
waivers, emergency planning and insurance.

**Berkeley rejects space reservations for these events without that approval.**
If an event might include high-school students, such as an outreach day or a
family open house, start here rather than with the room.

## Food and drink

- **Pre-packaged, shelf-stable snacks** at a table, such as chips and candy, need
  no permit. Tabling on Sproul counts as ordinary tabling rather than a public
  food event.
- **Bake sales are permitted and do need an EH&S permit**, under the campus bake
  sale guidelines.
- Serving anything else to the public requires an **EH&S event permit**. The
  organiser and everyone handling food need the **EHS 509 and EHS 510** trainings
  in the UC Learning Center. Permits are free, and new users email
  `ehstrain@berkeley.edu` for access.
- An RSO may hold a limited number of public food events per year.
- **Food trucks are prohibited** at RSO events.

## Waivers

Use the RSO waiver when participants do something that could injure them, or
handle equipment they would not normally use: races, tournaments, bounce houses,
sound systems and projectors with cables across a floor, trips off site, or anyone
under 18. OASIS holds the template, and Risk Services covers the rest at
<https://riskservices.berkeley.edu/student/events-activities>.

## Accessibility

Put accessibility information **on the invitation**: whether the venue is
step-free, where the nearest accessible bathroom is, whether a quiet room exists,
and whether food will be labelled. Checking this before booking the venue is
easier than changing it afterwards.

Berkeley's guidance for planning accessible events:
<https://dac.berkeley.edu/events/planning-accessible-events>.

If someone is harmed at an event, or discloses harm to an officer,
[getting support](../life/support-and-reporting.md) covers what to do.
