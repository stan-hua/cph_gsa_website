# Funding events

The GSA can draw on two sources of money. CPH administers its own fund, which is
the fastest route for most events. Berkeley adds campus funding that we can claim
as a registered graduate student organization.

## CPH's Community/Events Fund

CPH has a **Graduate Student Community/Events Fund** with a set annual budget, for
students who want to run events or buy resources for the CPH student community.
**Any CPH student can propose one**, GSA officer or otherwise.

The process, in order:

1. Talk through the idea with the GSAO first.
2. Complete the **Expense Request Form** and email it to the GSAO, Matt
   Malepeai (`mmalepe2@berkeley.edu`).
3. **Wait for approval.** Then buy.
4. Keep every receipt and invoice, and for events a guest list of attendees.
5. Submit for reimbursement with the documentation.

!!! danger "Approval first, no exceptions"
    Any expense claimed against this fund must have an approved Expense Request
    Form *before* the money is spent. The handbook says this explicitly and says
    there are no exceptions. CPH also cannot guarantee that a given reimbursement
    will be permitted, so check eligibility before committing to anything
    expensive.

## Campus money needs registration first

Everything below requires current registration and active
[signatories and financial agents](registration-and-signatories.md).

## Start with the Graduate Assembly

Every graduate student pays an ASUC fee each semester. The **Graduate Assembly**
allocates the graduate share of it, and it funds organizations made up primarily
of graduate students, which includes us.

**GRSO funding** covers meetings, events and general operations. It pays for
food, non-alcoholic drinks, compostable serving supplies and other event
supplies.

| Type | When to apply |
|---|---|
| Large Project Funding | **September or January** |
| Continual Funding, for ongoing events | **Monthly** |

!!! warning "GA funds require events open to the whole program"
    GA money cannot pay for internal-facing activities: board meetings, snacks
    for officers, or an event open to a single cohort or class year. A first-year
    mixer has to be open to the whole program, and a planning meeting does not
    qualify.

The GA also runs individual awards, including professional development and
**graduate student parent travel** awards.

Current guidelines, forms and deadlines: <https://ga.berkeley.edu/funding/>

## ASUC money requires sponsorship

ASUC funds and the ASUC's nonprofit status both require **ASUC sponsorship**,
which is separate from registration.

1. Add the **ASUC clause** to the constitution.
2. Submit the sponsorship form on CalLink
   (<https://callink.berkeley.edu/forms>).
3. Attend the **Senate Finance Committee** meeting where the application appears
   on the agenda. A GSA representative has to attend.
4. Wait for the Senate decision. Allow about a week and a half.

!!! warning "Sponsorship lapses every year"
    ASUC sponsorship requires **annual renewal**, and the Senate accepts
    applications only while it is in session, roughly **August to April**. An org
    that misses the window stays unsponsored until the following year, and loses
    access to the ASUC tax ID.

A sponsored org is covered by the ASUC's 501(c)(3), which allows outside donors to
give to us.

## The annual calendar

Berkeley allocates most campus money once a year through **ABSA**, the Annual
Budget and Space Allocation. The outgoing officers usually file for the incoming
year.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| February | Sponsorship, funding and space applications open on CalLink |
| Early March | Applications due |
| Mid-April | Initial awards announced |
| Late April | Appeal window |
| May | Senate passes the budget for the next year |
| August | Money becomes available, provided the org has re-registered |

Details and the current year's dates: <https://asuc.org/absa/>

The **ASUC Senate Contingency Fund** covers orgs that missed ABSA, formed
recently, or need more than they asked for. It accepts applications on CalLink
through the Fall and Spring semesters. The same conditions apply: ASUC
sponsorship, attendance at Finance Committee, and about a week and a half for a
decision.

## Raising our own money

Dues, sponsors and fundraisers are all permitted, under these rules:

- **Selling food requires an EH&S permit.** See
  [planning an event](../events/planning-an-event.md#food-and-drink).
- **Students and student groups may not act as sales representatives for an
  outside company.** Doing so can cost the org its sponsorship and its campus
  recognition. Buying shirts and reselling them for the org is permitted; acting
  as a channel for another company's product is prohibited.
- Fundraisers should be **one-off efforts**, clearly for the org's stated
  purpose, and run so that the group's benefit outweighs the commercial activity
  involved.
- A donor who wants proof of nonprofit status needs the **ASUC tax ID**,
  requested through a CalLink form. **Only sponsored orgs may request it.**
- [Give to Berkeley](https://give.berkeley.edu/home) and the
  [BIG GIVE](https://biggive.berkeley.edu/) campaign each March handle larger
  campaigns and online payments. Both require an appointment with the OASIS
  advisor first.
- Talk to the advisor before accepting any international gift.

Campus space and event charges are set out on rate sheets that change yearly, so
this page gives no figures.
[Planning an event](../events/planning-an-event.md#where-to-hold-it) links them.
