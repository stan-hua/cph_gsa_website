# FAQ

Questions that come up every year. If you asked something and had to hunt for the
answer, add it here.

??? question "Which campus am I actually a student at?"
    **In year 1, UC Berkeley** — you're matriculated there regardless of where
    you end up, unless you were admitted directly to a lab at UCSF.

    After year 1, you and your faculty advisor settle your **home campus**, and
    registration and enrolment move there.

    You'll have accounts on both campuses the whole time: a CalNet ID and
    CalCentral at UC Berkeley, MyAccess and the Student Portal at UCSF, plus an
    ID card for each.

    One consequence that bites later: **at the point you advance to candidacy,
    your primary advisor's home campus must match yours.** UC policy requires it,
    and the handbook makes it the student's responsibility to check. See
    [choosing an advisor](../academics/advisors.md#the-home-campus-trap).

??? question "Can I take courses at the other campus?"
    Yes — the program is built on it. Your required Cornerstone series, practicum,
    GRAD 202 and GRAD 214 are all **at UCSF**, while much of the computational
    coursework is at UC Berkeley. CPH students also get **priority registration**
    for a set of UC Berkeley courses in year 1, with academic advisor approval.

    Note that Berkeley runs semesters and UCSF runs quarters, so your term
    boundaries won't line up. Each term you need **12 semester units or 8 quarter
    units** total.

    !!! warning "Check both transcripts"
        Courses taken at UC Berkeley only reach your UCSF transcript once the two
        registrars exchange grades, and that lags. Review both periodically and
        raise anything missing with the GSAO.

??? question "How long does the program actually take?"
    **Normative time to degree is five years.** The official expectation is: pass
    the qualifying exam by the end of year 2, advance to candidacy immediately
    after, and file the dissertation by the end of year 5.

??? question "When exactly do I have to pass my qual?"
    **By 15 July of your second year.** That deadline exists so non-resident
    supplemental tuition can be waived for the following year.

    In practice, aim earlier — faculty availability collapses over the summer,
    and the handbook strongly encourages finishing before the close of spring
    semester in year 2. See [quals](../academics/quals.md).

??? question "What happens if I want to change advisors?"
    See [choosing an advisor](../academics/advisors.md). It's normal and the
    program has a process. Talk to the Head Graduate Advisor or the GSAO early.

??? question "Do I have to teach?"
    **Not according to the handbook**, which sets out no required teaching
    semesters and describes teaching only in optional terms. Treat it as
    something you do for the experience or the funding — but check your offer
    letter, which is the document that binds you.

    If you do want to, tell the GSAO no later than the end of the semester before
    the one you plan to teach in, and budget time for the four first-time GSI
    requirements at UC Berkeley. See
    [curriculum](../academics/curriculum.md#teaching).

??? question "Who do I talk to if something goes wrong with my advisor?"
    Several options, and none of them commits you to a formal process:

    - **Head Graduate Advisor** — Maya Petersen, CPH Co-Director. Also serves as
      backup when your own advisor is unavailable.
    - **GSAO** — Matt Malepeai, for anything administrative.
    - **CPH Equity Advisor** — Irene Chen, whose remit includes fairness in how
      students are treated and retained.
    - **Either campus's ombuds office** — confidential and independent.
    - Your GSA president.

??? question "How do I get access to the building after hours?"
    Your **Cal 1 Card** is the building-access card at UC Berkeley; at UCSF it's
    your **photo ID card**, which you're required to display at all times on UCSF
    premises. Request both as soon as your student profiles exist.

    For **BAIR Lab (BWW8)** access, email `bww8-facilities@berkeley.edu` and
    `cvalen@berkeley.edu`, cc'ing your faculty member — who must "reply all" with
    written approval. You'll get a form, then access.

??? question "Where do I sit?"
    Shared workspaces at both campuses: the CPH space in the Gateway at
    Berkeley, and 6th-floor cubicles at the Valley Center for Vision at Mission
    Bay. **First
    come, first served** — you don't need to be assigned one. Clear your desk at
    the end of the day. See [working across two campuses](../new-students/two-campuses.md#desks).
