# Start Here

If you've just been admitted or you're arriving this fall, read this page first.
It's the shortest path from "I accepted the offer" to "I know what I'm doing in
week one."

## The short version

A joint UC Berkeley–UCSF PhD means two of everything: two student IDs, two email
addresses, two library systems, two health plans to understand, two campuses to
get between. Nobody hands you a single checklist that covers both. This site is
our attempt at that checklist.

## Your first three months

=== "Before you arrive"

    - [ ] Accept the offer and complete both campuses' enrollment steps
    - [ ] Sort out housing — [start here](housing.md), and start early
    - [ ] Set up both email accounts and forward one to the other
    - [ ] Join the student Slack (ask the GSA for an invite)
    - [ ] Read [Before you arrive](before-you-arrive.md) in full

=== "Your first month"

    - [ ] Attend orientation at both campuses
    - [ ] Get both ID cards and building access
    - [ ] Meet your first-year advisor
    - [ ] Start rotations or lab conversations — see [advisors and labs](../academics/advisors.md)
    - [ ] Enroll in health insurance and pick a primary care provider

=== "By the end of semester one"

    - [ ] Confirm your coursework plan against the [curriculum requirements](../academics/curriculum.md)
    - [ ] Have a rough shortlist of labs
    - [ ] Know where your funding comes from and for how long — [funding](../academics/funding.md)
    - [ ] Go to at least one GSA social event. Seriously.

!!! warning "Deadlines differ between campuses"

    UC Berkeley and UCSF run on different academic calendars and different
    administrative deadlines. A deadline you hear about in a UC Berkeley meeting
    may not be the UCSF one. When in doubt, check both, and check the
    [forms and links](../resources/forms-and-links.md) page.

## Who to ask

| Question | Ask |
|---|---|
| Course requirements, milestones | Your program's graduate student services advisor |
| "Is this normal?" | A second- or third-year student — that's what the GSA is for |
| Funding, payroll, reimbursements | Program administrator at the campus paying you |
| Visa and international student issues | The international office at your home campus |
| Something's gone badly wrong | Your program head, or the ombuds office at either campus |
