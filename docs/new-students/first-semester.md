# Your first semester

## What the semester actually looks like

Coursework takes less time than you expect and rotations take more. Most people
find the hard part is not the work but the ambiguity — no one tells you whether
you're on track, because there isn't a single track.

## A reasonable rhythm

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Orientation, enrollment, meeting people. Say yes to things. |
| 3–8 | First rotation. Read broadly. Start narrowing lab interests. |
| 8–12 | Second rotation or deepening the first. Talk to senior students in those labs. |
| 12–16 | Finals, and a real conversation with your advisor about year two. |

## Common first-semester mistakes

!!! failure "Waiting to be told"
    Deadlines, funding decisions, and rotation slots move on people who ask.
    Asking early is not pushy here; it's the norm.

!!! failure "Optimizing coursework"
    Grades matter far less than the relationships you build in your rotations.
    Do the work, but don't trade lab time for a better grade.

!!! failure "Isolating"
    The students who leave are rarely the ones who struggled with the material.
    Go to the GSA events. Keep one non-PhD thing in your life.
