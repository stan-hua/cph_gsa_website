# Your first semester

## What the semester actually looks like

Coursework takes less time than you expect and rotations take more. Most people
find the hard part is not the work but the ambiguity — no one tells you whether
you're on track, because there isn't a single track.

## Getting registered

In year 1 you register at **UC Berkeley**, unless you were admitted directly to a
lab at UCSF.

Two things have to be true for you to count as officially registered:

1. You're enrolled in **at least one class**, and
2. **At least 20% of tuition and fees** has been paid by the due date.

Enrolment happens through **CalCentral** at UC Berkeley (there's a *CalCentral:
Course Enrollment* video worth ten minutes) and through the **Student Portal** at
UCSF.

!!! warning "A hold will silently block you"
    If there's an active hold on your account you cannot register at all. It
    won't resolve itself — contact the GSAO.

!!! tip "Book a GSAO meeting in week 1"
    The handbook explicitly encourages it, and it's the single highest-value hour
    of the semester: confirm your fellowship or appointment tuition disbursement
    is actually flowing, sanity-check your class schedule, and ask everything
    you've been saving up.

## What you're enrolled in

Every term you need **12 semester units or 8 quarter units** — required courses,
electives and research units combined.

Your first year is mostly fixed:

| Course | What | Campus |
|---|---|---|
| CPH 200A / 200B / 200C | Cornerstone series — mandatory, CPH students only | UCSF |
| CPH 270 | CPH Doctoral Seminar (two quarters of it must fall in year 1) | UCB or UCSF |
| GRAD 202 | Race and Racism in Science | UCSF, fall quarter |
| GRAD 214 / RCR | Responsible conduct of research | UCSF |

Plus electives chosen with your academic advisor — CPH students get **priority
registration** for a set of UC Berkeley courses in year 1. Full list on the
[curriculum page](../academics/curriculum.md#electives).

## Rotations

**Rotation 1 is assigned before fall semester even starts**, by mutual agreement
between you and the faculty member. Direct Admit students do this rotation with
their primary advisor. A rotation runs a semester or a quarter — not a few weeks.

If you weren't Direct Admit, and you and your rotation 1 host don't both decide to
continue, the advising team helps you find rotations 2 and 3. CPH funds **up to
three**. See [choosing an advisor](../academics/advisors.md#rotations).

## A reasonable rhythm

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| Before term | Rotation 1 confirmed. Meet your first-year academic advisor. |
| 1–3 | Orientation, enrolment, GSAO meeting. Say yes to things. |
| 3–10 | Rotation 1 in earnest, alongside Cornerstone and seminar. |
| 10–14 | Talk to senior students in labs you're considering next. |
| 14–16 | Finals, and a real conversation about whether rotation 1 continues. |

## Before summer: California residency

If you're a US citizen you're **expected to establish California residency after
your first year**, and CalCentral and the Student Portal will ask for documents
the summer *before* year 2.

This is not optional paperwork — if you skip it you keep paying non-resident
tuition. You have to satisfy the "physical presence" and "intent to remain in
California" requirements by the residence determination date, which is the first
day of instruction. Raise questions with the GSAO early.

International students can't establish residency and instead incur **Non-Resident
Supplemental Tuition (NRST)** each semester. CPH may cover NRST for up to two
years, as set out in your offer letter, and the Graduate Division waives it for up
to three calendar years after you advance to candidacy. This is one of the reasons
the [qual deadline](../academics/quals.md#timing-and-eligibility) is 15 July of
year 2.

## Common first-semester mistakes

!!! failure "Waiting to be told"
    Deadlines, funding decisions, and rotation slots move on people who ask.
    Asking early is not pushy here; it's the norm.

!!! failure "Optimizing coursework"
    Grades matter far less than the relationships you build in your rotations.
    Do the work, but don't trade lab time for a better grade. (That said, you
    need a **3.0 GPA** to sit your qual.)

!!! failure "Isolating"
    The students who leave are rarely the ones who struggled with the material.
    Go to the GSA events. Keep one non-PhD thing in your life.
