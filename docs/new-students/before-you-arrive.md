# Before you arrive

Everything on this page can be done from wherever you are now, and doing it early
saves a genuinely stressful first week.

## Accepting the offer takes two forms

This is the step people miss, because the second form isn't on the campus that
admitted you.

1. **Reply to Offer of Admission**, and submit the **Statement of Intent to
   Register (SIR)** online. Only the SIR reserves your place. It's also what
   triggers matriculation and gets you into university systems.
2. **UCSF Supplemental Applicant Form, due by 1 June.** This is what creates
   your student profile at UCSF and unlocks UCSF campus resources.

!!! warning "You matriculate at UC Berkeley in year 1"
    Whichever campus you end up calling home, in year 1 you matriculate at UC
    Berkeley, unless you were admitted directly to a lab at UCSF. The UCSF form is
    what makes you visible on the other campus.

New students are matriculated by the Graduate Admissions Office by **1 June**,
before their first semester. After that you'll have access to UC Berkeley's
**CalCentral** and UCSF's **Student Portal**.

## Accounts and access

Once matriculated you receive your **Student Identification Number (SID)**. That
one number is what you use to set up everything else:

- A **berkeley.edu email address and CalNet ID**, which gate bCourses, campus
  wifi, library access and most UC Berkeley forms
- A **Cal 1 Card** (see below)
- Access to **CalCentral**

On the UCSF side you'll have **MyAccess**, which gates UCSF email, APeX training
if your work touches clinical data, and UCSF library resources.

Pick one address as your working address and forward the other to it.

!!! tip
    Set up two-factor on both accounts now, on a phone you'll still have in
    September. Recovering a locked account across two institutions is slow.

## ID cards: request both, early

You need physical access to both campuses, so request both cards as soon as your
student profiles exist in CalCentral and the Student Portal.

| Campus | Card | Book an appointment |
|---|---|---|
| UC Berkeley | **Cal 1 Card**: building access, debit account, meal plans, university services | [cal1card.berkeley.edu](https://cal1card.berkeley.edu/get-a-cal-1-card/) |
| UCSF | **UCSF photo ID card** | [registrar.ucsf.edu](https://registrar.ucsf.edu/new-students/weid) |

!!! note "UCSF ID must be visible"
    You're required to display your UCSF ID at all times on premises UCSF owns or
    operates. It isn't just for door readers.

## Your CalCentral task list

CalCentral lists tasks and deadlines on the dashboard. Work through all of them,
including:

- **SVSH mandatory training**
- **Immunization and TB screening**
- Anything else carrying a deadline

## Mailing lists

Once matriculated, sign up for **CalMessages** at UC Berkeley. UCSF adds you to
its student listserv automatically. Policy changes, events, workshops and
fellowship announcements go there.

## Paperwork

- I-9 and payroll setup at whichever campus is paying you
- Health insurance: you're **automatically enrolled in SHIP** and charged for it.
  You can waive it with comparable major medical cover, and the waiver deadline is
  real. See [wellness](../life/wellness.md)
- International students: visa documents, SEVIS, and arrival check-in
- Any background or immunization requirements if your research is clinical

## Orientation

The Graduate Division runs a **New Graduate Student Orientation (NGSO)** at UC
Berkeley during the first week of the fall semester. Dates go up on the Graduate
Division website.

## Other things to do

- **Email a few current students.** <span class="todo">Say who can put an
  incoming student in touch.</span>
- **Read two or three papers from labs you might join.** We recommend arriving at
  those conversations with a specific question.
- **Sort out a bank account and phone plan early** if you're moving from abroad;
  both are needed for a lease.
- **Know who your first-year academic advisor is.** They're assigned at
  matriculation, they are not your research advisor, and you meet twice a
  semester, with you doing the scheduling. See
  [choosing an advisor](../academics/advisors.md).
