# Choosing an advisor and lab

The single most consequential decision of your PhD, made with the least
information, in your first year. Here's how to make it less blind.

## Questions worth asking a prospective advisor

- How often do we meet, and is it scheduled or ad hoc?
- What happens when a project isn't working — how long before we change course?
- How do you decide authorship?
- What have your last few students done after graduating?
- How is my funding secured, and for how long?

## Questions worth asking their students

Ask these separately, and ask more than one student.

- What's the feedback like on drafts, and how fast?
- What happens when someone wants to leave for industry?
- Has anyone in the lab been unhappy, and what happened?
- Would you choose this lab again?

## Signals to weigh

!!! success "Good signs"
    Students graduate in reasonable time. Former students speak well of the
    advisor years later. The advisor says "I don't know" in lab meeting.

!!! danger "Worth a hard look"
    High student turnover with vague explanations. Nobody in the lab takes
    vacation. You cannot get a straight answer about funding.
