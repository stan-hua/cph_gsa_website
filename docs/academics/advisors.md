# Choosing an advisor and lab

What the program requires of each advising role, and what to ask before choosing
a primary advisor.

## You will have more than one advisor

CPH separates the roles, so different questions go to different people.

| Role | Who assigns it | When |
|---|---|---|
| **First-year academic advisor** | The program, at matriculation | Day one |
| **Primary (research) advisor** / dissertation chair | You, via rotations, or at admission if Direct Admit | Year 1–2 |
| **Co-primary advisors** (optional) | You and both advisors, by mutual agreement | Any time |
| **CPH interdisciplinary mentor** | You, with your primary advisor | End of year 1 to mid-year 3 |

### First-year academic advisor

Assigned to every incoming student at matriculation, matched on background, and
drawn from CPH Type 1 faculty. They handle course selection, navigating program
requirements, and helping non-Direct-Admit students find rotations.

**They are explicitly not your research mentor**, and the program expects nothing
more from the relationship. A Direct Admit student's academic advisor is a
*different person* from the faculty member who admitted them.

You meet **at least twice per semester** during year 1, and the handbook puts the
scheduling on you. Come with real questions: course choices, faculty you want
introductions to, labs you are eyeing for rotations 2 and 3.

### Primary (research) advisor / dissertation chair

Your research mentor from funding through to dissertation completion. Only **core
CPH AGG faculty (Type 1 or Type 2)** are eligible.

When it gets formalised, in writing:

- **Direct Admit students:** at the time of admission. Your first rotation is
  with this advisor.
- **Everyone else:** no later than the **start of spring semester or winter
  quarter of year 2**, identified through rotations. The Head Graduate Advisor and
  GSAO decide later timelines case by case.

What they owe you:

- **Funding you at the committed level until the end of year 5 or graduation**,
  whichever comes first, typically GSR appointments at no less than 50% FTE
- Research mentorship and help developing the dissertation
- Help building your qual committee and getting you through the exam by end of
  year 2
- Guidance ahead of GSI/TA hiring deadlines, if you want or need to teach

!!! warning "Your advisor cannot chair your qual"
    The primary advisor, and either co-chair, is barred from chairing your
    qualifying exam committee. Plan the committee around that.

### Co-primary advisors

You and two advisors can mutually agree to co-chair. Tell the GSAO and update
your committee in CalCentral or the Student Portal via the appropriate eForm.
Co-advisors are **jointly and severally responsible** under Graduate Division
policy, so each holds the full obligation rather than half of it.

### CPH interdisciplinary mentor, required and easy to forget

At least one is **required**. If your primary advisor is mainly a health
scientist, your interdisciplinary mentor is mainly a computational scientist, and
vice versa. Often they're on the opposite
campus from your primary advisor. Usually CPH faculty, core or affiliate, but
that isn't required.

You meet at least twice per semester, and they give feedback on your research
trajectory and career, not just the current project.

!!! danger "There is a form, and it has a deadline"
    The interdisciplinary mentor form is due by the **end of fall semester in
    year 3**. It's the single easiest requirement in the program to let slide,
    because nothing breaks until it does.

## Rotations

CPH funds **up to three lab rotations** to help you find a primary advisor.

Students admitted directly to a lab work with their primary advisor from the
start, so the rotation sequence below applies to everyone else.

- **Rotation 1** is assigned before the start of fall semester in year 1, by
  mutual agreement between you and the faculty member. Direct Admit students do
  this rotation with their primary advisor.
- **Rotations 2 and 3** are for students who weren't Direct Admit, or who aren't
  continuing with their rotation 1 advisor. The advising team helps you find them.

See [funding](funding.md) for how rotation support works. It depends on whether
you were Direct Admit, and the first-semester support can be deferred.

## Questions to ask a prospective advisor

- How often do we meet, and is it scheduled or ad hoc?
- What happens when a project stalls, and how long before we change course?
- How do you decide authorship?
- What have your last few students done after graduating?
- How is my funding secured, and for how long?

## Questions to ask their students

Ask these separately, and ask more than one student.

- What's the feedback like on drafts, and how fast?
- What happens when someone wants to leave for industry?
- Has anyone in the lab been unhappy, and what happened?
- Would you choose this lab again?

## Home campus

When you advance to candidacy, **your primary advisor's home campus must be the
same as yours.** UC policy requires it, and the handbook makes it the student's
responsibility rather than the program's.

Raise this with a prospective advisor *before* you commit, rather than in year 3
when it becomes a problem. Ask the GSAO early what changing home campus involves if
you are considering an advisor on the other campus.

## If it isn't working

Changing labs is normal, and the program has done it before. Talk to the Head
Graduate Advisor or the GSAO early. The Head Graduate Advisor also serves as backup
when your own advisor is unavailable.
