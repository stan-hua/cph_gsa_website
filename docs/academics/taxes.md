# Taxes

Graduate student income is taxed in ways that catch people out: fellowship money
is often paid without withholding, the two campuses use different payroll systems,
and international students file on a different set of forms from everyone else.

!!! warning "Nobody here can give you tax advice"
    This page describes the process and points at the campus resources. It is not
    tax advice, and a GSA officer cannot check your return. For anything specific
    to your situation, use the campus resources below or a tax professional.

## Wages and fellowships are treated differently

| Income | Withholding | Form you receive |
|---|---|---|
| GSR or GSI wages | Yes, withheld from each paycheck | **W-2** |
| Fellowship or traineeship stipend | Often none | <span class="todo">Confirm what CPH fellowship recipients receive, and when</span> |

Federal and California returns are both due in **mid-April**, for the previous
calendar year. UCSF gives **15 April 2026** as the deadline for 2025 returns.

## If you are an international student

Your filing status depends on how long you have been in the US and on your visa,
and it changes what you file and which software you can use.

### GLACIER, first

UC uses **GLACIER**, an online tax compliance system, to work out your tax
residency and whether a tax treaty applies to you. Students go through it before
filing, and you will normally be asked to complete a GLACIER record when the
University first pays you.

- Complete it promptly. Until it is done, the University may withhold at the
  highest rate.
- Keep the summary and the forms it produces. You need them at filing time.
- GLACIER is separate from filing a return. Completing it does not file anything
  on your behalf.

### What each campus provides

Both campuses buy nonresident filing software and give it to students at no
charge, and **both cover the federal return only**. The California return is
yours to arrange either way.

| Campus | Software | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| UC Berkeley | **GLACIER Tax Prep (GTP)**, via Berkeley International Office | Request access through the form BIO posts in February. If UC Berkeley sent you a 1042-S, reach GTP through your GLACIER record instead |
| UCSF | **GLACIER Tax Prep**, via the International Students and Scholars Office | Submit the ISSO eligibility form. ISSO replies within 48 business hours |

Eligibility is conditional at UC Berkeley, and depends on having had UCB
employment, CPT or OPT authorisation, an I-House scholarship or similar during the
tax year. UCSF asks that you were a student, scholar or affiliate during the tax
year. Check the pages linked below for the current year's terms.

!!! warning "Neither campus pays for Sprintax"
    Sprintax is a paid product here. Some universities buy access codes for their
    students, and UC Berkeley and UCSF buy GLACIER Tax Prep instead, so you would
    be paying Sprintax yourself.

### Filing the return

Nonresident filers use **Form 1040-NR** rather than the 1040, and many are also
required to file **Form 8843**, including people with no US income at all.

Most consumer filing services handle resident returns only. These three handle the
nonresident forms:

| Option | Cost | Electronic filing |
|---|---|---|
| **GLACIER Tax Prep** | Free through either campus | None. It prepares the return for you to print, sign and post |
| **Sprintax** | Paid | Federal return only, and not in every case. State returns have to be printed and posted |
| **OLT**, [olt.com](https://www.olt.com/) | Around $10 per state return, federal from free | Supports e-filing both federal and California returns, including the nonresident forms |

GLACIER Tax Prep and Sprintax both prepare the 1040-NR and Form 8843 and run the
treaty analysis. OLT is a general filing service that supports the nonresident
forms rather than a product built around them.

### Filing electronically, or by post

The free campus software does not file anything for you. GLACIER Tax Prep prepares
the return, and you print it, sign it and post it to the IRS.

Sprintax can e-file the **federal** return, though it excludes a long list of
situations: filing with an ITIN rather than an SSN, self-employment income, capital
gains, several of the 1099 forms, claiming academic expenses, and a return with no
gross income. **Sprintax never e-files a state return**, so California goes in an
envelope.

OLT e-files the federal and California returns together. It does not e-file a state
return on its own, so submit both at the same time.

### Tax treaties

The US has tax treaties with many countries, and some exempt part of a student's
income from federal tax. What you are eligible for depends on your country, and
GLACIER applies any benefit you qualify for.
