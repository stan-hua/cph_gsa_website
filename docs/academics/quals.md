# Qualifying exam

The qual is the milestone students ask about most and the one about which the
official documentation says least. This page is the students' version, with the
formal rules pulled out of the *CPH Graduate Student Handbook 2026–2027* so you
don't have to go hunting for them.

## Format

A single oral exam, **up to three hours**, with no separate written proposal —
though you will be presenting a written research plan's worth of material.

It runs like this:

1. **A 45-minute presentation** of your dissertation topic: a review of previous
   work and a proposed research strategy. You need to present at least one
   primary dissertation project or chapter in depth — **work in progress is
   explicitly acceptable** — and then outline future directions and where the
   dissertation as a whole is going.
2. **Questions, during and after.** The committee is testing whether you can
   synthesise what you've learned from courses and research and apply it to
   problems *they* pose. Expect questions well outside your dissertation's focus:
   methodology, theory, implementation, application, deployment considerations,
   and why any of it matters.

You're assessed on four things: depth in your research area, breadth in the
fundamentals of computational precision health, your ability to formulate a
research plan, and critical thinking.

The exam formally covers three areas:

1. Fundamentals of **computational science**
2. Fundamentals of **health science**
3. Your chosen **third area** of research specialisation

!!! tip "The third area is yours to define"
    It's the one part of the exam you control. Choose it deliberately, and make
    sure your committee agrees on what it covers before exam day.

## Timing and eligibility

The QE must happen **no later than 15 July of your second year** — that deadline
exists so that non-resident supplemental tuition can be waived for the following
year.

!!! warning "Aim for spring, not July"
    Faculty availability collapses over the summer. The handbook strongly
    encourages scheduling before the close of spring semester in year 2. If you
    aim at 15 July and one committee member goes away, you have no slack left.

You need a **GPA of at least 3.0** to sit the exam.

If you're also earning a Designated Emphasis, you must be admitted to it *before*
you sit the qual.

## Choosing a committee

**Four members**, all from the Berkeley or UCSF Academic Senates:

- **Three CPH AGG core faculty**
- **One Academic Senate Representative** ("outside member") from another
  department — statistics, epidemiology, bioengineering and so on. They may be a
  CPH AGG Affiliate, but they must be a Senate member from **your** home campus.
  Not all AGG Affiliates are Senate members, so check.

Three constraints that catch people out:

- At least one faculty member **from each campus** must be on the committee.
- The **chair** must be CPH AGG core faculty with the **same home campus as you**.
- Your **primary research advisor cannot chair** the qual — nor can a dissertation
  co-chair.

Building the committee is your job, in consultation with your primary advisor,
and it has to be approved by the Head Graduate Advisor and your home campus
Graduate Division. Work with the GSAO to get the application in early enough.

If you're doing a Designated Emphasis, one DE faculty member must serve on both
your qual and dissertation committees, and the Graduate Division needs to approve
the committee at least **three weeks** before the exam.

Beyond the rules: ask your advisor for suggestions, then ask senior students what
those people are actually like in an exam. Both inputs matter. A committee member
who asks hard questions in a supportive way is worth more than one who asks none.

## The paperwork

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| 1 | Form your committee (see above) |
| 2 | Find a date and a **three-hour** block — a Doodle poll across four faculty calendars is worth the effort |
| 3 | File it: at UC Berkeley, the Higher Degree Committees eForm via CalCentral → My Dashboard → Student Resources; at UCSF, the qualifying exam application in the Student Portal |

The UCSF application is due **at least two weeks before** the exam. File earlier
than the minimum — approvals take time, and a committee that isn't approved is a
committee that can't examine you.

## Remote and hybrid exams

Allowed, either fully remote or hybrid, but **only with your consent** — the
program has to get your agreement on the format before the exam is held.

One rule worth knowing in advance: **if the exam is hybrid and you are physically
present, the chair must be physically present too.** Exceptions need Head
Graduate Advisor approval *before* the exam.

The whole committee must be present for the entire exam. You cannot be examined
by committee members separately. If someone can't attend in any form, the exam is
rescheduled or the committee reconstituted — the Graduate Division can expedite
that, via the GSAO.

## Working backwards

Not program policy, just what tends to work:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 6 months before | Choose committee members, ask them in person |
| 4 months before | Circulate a one-page outline of the proposal |
| 2 months before | Full draft to your advisor |
| 4 weeks before | Draft to the full committee |
| 2 weeks before | Practice talk in front of lab mates. Do this. |

## Results

The committee grades the exam **Pass**, **Fail**, or a middle outcome that is
named differently on each campus: **Contingent Pass** at UCSF, pending your
response to the committee's concerns about the proposal, or **Partial Failure**
at UC Berkeley.

You normally find out the same day.

## If it doesn't go well

Not passing on the first attempt happens, and it is recoverable. The formal
process:

- You may retake the exam **once**.
- It must be with the **same committee**.
- There's a **minimum of three months** between the two exams.
- Failing the second exam is grounds for termination from the PhD program, and
  that decision is made in consultation with your home campus Graduate Division.

Talk to the GSAO and your advisor early rather than late — the three-month
minimum means the calendar is working against you from the moment the first exam
ends.

## After you pass

Advance to candidacy as soon as your dissertation committee is settled. You must
file the Application for Candidacy **no later than the end of the semester after
the one in which you passed**, and there's a **$90 advancement fee**.

One thing to check at this point: your primary advisor's home campus must be the
same as yours at the time you advance. UC policy requires it, and it's *your*
responsibility to make sure it lines up.
