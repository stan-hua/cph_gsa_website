# Qualifying exam

The formal rules for the qualifying exam, from the *CPH Graduate Student Handbook
2026–2027*.

## Format

A single oral exam of **up to three hours**, with no separate written proposal.
You still present a written research plan's worth of material.

It runs like this:

1. **A 45-minute presentation** of your dissertation topic: a review of previous
   work and a proposed research strategy. You need to present at least one
   primary dissertation project or chapter in depth (**work in progress is
   explicitly acceptable**), then outline future directions and where the
   dissertation as a whole is going.
2. **Questions, during and after.** The committee tests whether you can
   synthesise what you have learned and apply it to problems *they* pose. Expect
   questions well outside your dissertation's focus: methodology, theory,
   implementation, application, deployment, and why any of it matters.

You're assessed on four things: depth in your research area, breadth in the
fundamentals of computational precision health, your ability to formulate a
research plan, and critical thinking.

The exam formally covers three areas:

1. Fundamentals of **computational science**
2. Fundamentals of **health science**
3. Your chosen **third area** of research specialisation

!!! tip "The third area is yours to define"
    You control this one part of the exam. Choose it deliberately, and agree with
    your committee on what it covers before exam day.

## Timing and eligibility

The QE must happen **no later than 15 July of your second year**. That deadline
exists so that non-resident supplemental tuition can be waived for the following
year.

!!! warning "Aim for spring, not July"
    Faculty are largely unavailable over the summer, so the handbook encourages
    scheduling before the close of spring semester in year 2. At 15 July, one
    committee member's holiday leaves no slack.

You need a **GPA of at least 3.0** to sit the exam.

If you're also earning a Designated Emphasis, you must be admitted to it *before*
you sit the qual.

## Choosing a committee

**Four members**, all from the Berkeley or UCSF Academic Senates:

- **Three CPH AGG core faculty**
- **One Academic Senate Representative** ("outside member") from another
  department (statistics, epidemiology, bioengineering). They may be a CPH AGG
  Affiliate, and they must be a Senate member from **your** home campus. Some AGG
  Affiliates sit outside the Senate, so check.

Three constraints that catch people out:

- At least one faculty member **from each campus** must be on the committee.
- The **chair** must be CPH AGG core faculty with the **same home campus as you**.
- Your **primary research advisor cannot chair** the qual, and neither can a
  dissertation co-chair.

Building the committee is your job, in consultation with your primary advisor,
and it has to be approved by the Head Graduate Advisor and your home campus
Graduate Division. Work with the GSAO to get the application in early enough.

If you're doing a Designated Emphasis, one DE faculty member must serve on both
your qual and dissertation committees, and the Graduate Division needs to approve
the committee at least **three weeks** before the exam.

For suggestions, ask your advisor, and ask senior students what a given faculty
member is like in an exam.

## The paperwork

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| 1 | Form your committee (see above) |
| 2 | Find a date and a **three-hour** block. We recommend a Doodle poll across the four calendars |
| 3 | File it: at UC Berkeley, the Higher Degree Committees eForm via CalCentral → My Dashboard → Student Resources; at UCSF, the qualifying exam application in the Student Portal |

The UCSF application is due **at least two weeks before** the exam. File earlier
than that, because approvals take time and an unapproved committee cannot examine
you.

## Remote and hybrid exams

Both fully remote and hybrid exams are allowed, **only with your consent**. The
program has to agree the format with you before the exam.

One rule to plan around: **if the exam is hybrid and you are physically present,
the chair must be physically present too.** Exceptions need Head
Graduate Advisor approval *before* the exam.

The whole committee must be present for the entire exam, and members cannot
examine you separately. If someone cannot attend in any form, the exam is
rescheduled or the committee reconstituted. The Graduate Division can expedite
that, via the GSAO.

## Results

The committee grades the exam **Pass**, **Fail**, or a middle outcome that is
named differently on each campus: **Contingent Pass** at UCSF, pending your
response to the committee's concerns about the proposal, or **Partial Failure**
at UC Berkeley.

You normally find out the same day.

## If it doesn't go well

Failing the first attempt happens, and it is recoverable. The formal process:

- You may retake the exam **once**.
- The retake uses the **same committee**.
- **At least three months** separate the two exams.
- Failing the second exam is grounds for termination from the PhD program, and
  that decision is made in consultation with your home campus Graduate Division.

Talk to the GSAO and your advisor early. The three-month minimum puts the
calendar against you from the moment the first exam ends.

## After you pass

Advance to candidacy as soon as your dissertation committee is settled. You must
file the Application for Candidacy **no later than the end of the semester after
the one in which you passed**, and there's a **$90 advancement fee**.

Check one thing at this point: your primary advisor's home campus must match
yours when you advance. UC policy requires it, and the responsibility is *yours*.
