# Curriculum and requirements

Summarised from the official *CPH Graduate Student Handbook 2026–2027*. This page
exists so you can see the shape of the degree on one screen; the handbook is the
document that binds you.

## At a glance

| Milestone | Timing |
|---|---|
| Core coursework complete | Four semesters / six quarters — end of year 2 |
| Qualifying exam | By 15 July of year 2 — see [quals](quals.md) |
| Advancement to candidacy | By the end of the semester **after** you pass |
| Dissertation filed | End of year 5 (normative time to degree) |

Normative time to degree is **five years**.

## The unit requirement

You need at least **26 units of academic coursework**, plus at least **20 units**
of practicum, doctoral seminar and research rotations. Coursework has to span
both the computational and health sciences, and has to happen on both campuses.

The curriculum normally takes four semesters or six quarters. A fifth semester
(summer quarter) is sometimes needed, at your faculty advisor's discretion.

Years 1–2 are coursework plus rotations. From year 3 on, units are normally
dissertation research.

!!! note "Enrolment minimum"
    Every term you must be enrolled in **12 semester units or 8 quarter units** —
    any mix of required courses, electives and research units.

## Required coursework

### Year 1

| Course | What | Campus | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPH 200A / 200B / 200C | Cornerstone series (3 units × 3 quarters) | UCSF | Fall, Winter, Spring quarters |
| CPH 270 | CPH Doctoral Seminar (2 units) | UCB (Fall + Spring semesters) or UCSF (each quarter) | Ongoing |
| GRAD 202 | Race and Racism in Science (2 quarter units ≈ 1.3 semester units) | UCSF | Fall quarter |
| GRAD 214 | Ethics and the Responsible Conduct of Research (1.5 quarter units ≈ 1 semester unit) | UCSF | — |

The Cornerstone series is small interdisciplinary teams working through four
projects — data analysis, problem identification and ideation, and integrating
CPH into care and research contexts. It's open only to CPH PhD students.

### Year 2

| Course | What | Campus | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPH 201A / 201B | CPH Practicum (2 units × 2 quarters) | UCSF | Two quarters |
| CPH 270 | CPH Doctoral Seminar, continuing | UCB or UCSF | Each semester / quarter |

The practicum is deliberately **outside the classroom** — hospitals, primary care
clinics, health insurance companies, federally qualified health centres,
community vaccination sites, local and state health departments — supplemented by
virtual presentations. Placements are matched to the cohort's interests.

### The seminar, across the whole degree

CPH 270 is not a one-year course. You need **at least six quarters (three
semesters)** of it, **two of which must be in your first year**.

### Responsible Conduct of Research

Separate from GRAD 214, you must complete RCR training. At UC Berkeley this is
the [CITI Program](https://about.citiprogram.org/) curriculum through the Office
of Research Administration and Compliance; UCSF runs its own RCR course.

!!! warning "One course, two numbers"
    The handbook lists the year-1 RCR requirement as "RCR Course (MCB #)" in one
    place and `GRAD 214` in another. Check with the GSAO which applies to your
    cohort before you enrol.

## Research units

Independent research is **CPH 299** at UC Berkeley and **CPH 250** at UCSF. Enrol
only with your academic advisor's permission, and register under the specific
faculty member supervising the work — they're the one who grades it. If your
supervisor isn't listed under a CPH 299 section, ask the GSAO.

!!! tip "Check your transcript, twice a year"
    Courses you take at UC Berkeley only appear on your UCSF transcript once the
    two registrars exchange grades. This lags. Review both transcripts
    periodically and raise anything missing with the GSAO — much easier to fix in
    the same year than three years later.

## Electives

Courses are chosen with your academic advisor. Most are graduate level, but
because students arrive with very different backgrounds, some upper-division
undergraduate courses are permitted in areas where you have less preparation.

CPH students hold **priority registration** for a set of UC Berkeley courses in
the first year, with academic advisor approval:

| Course | Units |

| CS 182 / CS 282A — Designing, Visualizing and Understanding Deep Neural Networks | 4 |

| CS 189 — Introduction to Machine Learning | 4 |
| CS 285 — Deep Reinforcement Learning, Decision Making and Control | 3 |
| CS 288 — Natural Language Processing | 3 |
| CS C280 — Computer Vision | 3 |
| CS 281A / STAT C241A — Statistical Learning Theory | 3 |
| EECS 227B — Convex Optimization | 3 |

New courses are added every year, so treat this as a starting point rather than
the full menu.

## Teaching

**The handbook sets out no required teaching semesters.** It describes teaching
in optional terms throughout — "in cases when the student wants/needs to teach" —
so treat it as something you do for the experience or the funding, not a
milestone. If your offer letter says otherwise, your offer letter wins.

If you do want to teach, tell the GSAO **no later than the end of the semester
before** the one you plan to teach in. Appointments are 50% (20 hrs/week) or 25%
(10 hrs/week), and student academic appointments are capped at 50% so that you
stay at least half-time on your own degree.

First-time GSIs at UC Berkeley must complete four things before they can work
with students:

- A day-long Teaching Conference for first-time GSIs
- The online course *GSI Professional Standards and Ethics*
- A 300-level semester-long pedagogy course
- An hour-long Academic Student Employee (ASE) orientation

Open GSI and GSR positions outside CPH are listed on Handshake and on the
Graduate Division's appointment announcement page. At UCSF, teaching happens
through the professional schools and through partnerships with SFSU, CCSF, UC
Berkeley Extension and Mt. Tamalpais College.

See [funding](funding.md) for what these appointments pay.

## Designated Emphasis

CPH also offers a Designated Emphasis for graduate students already enrolled in
other programs at either campus. If you're pursuing a DE alongside your PhD you
must be admitted to it **before** sitting your qualifying exam, and a DE faculty
member must sit on both your qual and dissertation committees.
